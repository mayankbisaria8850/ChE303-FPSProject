CONCLUSIONS

What we found, and why it matters to customers

Key findings

  • A 1 t/h line turns waste into 167 kg/h of dry fibre and 449 kg/h of juice.
  • The screen cut size sets the fibre–juice trade-off; feed scales everything linearly.
  • Python models reproduce all 42 Aspen cases, so the website runs without Aspen.

Competitive differentiation

  • Two products from one waste stream, on site at the juice plant.
  • Size-controlled fibre: customers choose the cut size for their product mix.
  • An engineering calculator lets customers test their own throughput first.

ZestLoop · Conclusions

Three findings: the process converts most of the waste into saleable product; the screen is the single design knob, trading fibre against juice, while capacity scales linearly; and the Python models match Aspen exactly, which is what lets the website work offline. Differentiation: link these points to the gaps in the competitor survey, for example that competitors sell only equipment, or only one product.
