COMPANY AND PROBLEM

Half of every orange becomes a waste problem

45–50 %

of the fruit mass leaves a juice plant as wet pomace: peel, pulp, seeds and trapped juice.

Why it hurts the juice plant

  • About 80 % liquid and full of fermentable sugar, so it spoils and smells within hours.
  • Plants pay to truck it to landfill or sell it as low-value cattle feed.
  • The juice and dietary fibre locked inside are lost product.

OUR CUSTOMER

Citrus juice and pulp processors in the Malwa orange belt of Madhya Pradesh and across India, each producing roughly 0.5–2 t/h of wet pomace in season.

ZestLoop · Company and problem

The problem: juice extraction leaves 45 to 50 percent of the fruit as pomace (figure from our Aspen report's literature basis). It is about 80 percent liquid with sugars, so it ferments fast and costs the plant money to dispose of. Our customer is the juice or pulp processor that generates this stream, typically 0.5 to 2 tonnes per hour during the season. Our design basis is 1 tonne per hour.
